<a id="L0002"></a>
[L0002] An interpolation kernel's L1 norm bounds sample peaks, not loop gain — check |H(e^jω)| for stability | candidate | added: 2026-10-19 | tags: feedback-stability, time-accuracy | lesson: The small-gain stability bound for a feedback loop concerns the frequency response |g·H|, while the sum of |taps| bounds the worst-case sample peak (it matters to a ceiling's headroom or a peak-based test). 4-point Lagrange/Catmull-Rom have sum|taps| = 1.25 at a half-sample offset yet max |H| = 1.000, so they do not threaten loop stability. Conflating the two either rejects a safe kernel or mis-sizes the ceiling. Sibling of ADR-142's "peak amplitude is NOT loop gain". | evidence: a research agent claimed "peak gain up to 1.25" as a stability risk; the lead recomputed both kernels over offsets 0..1 (numpy, 4001-point grid): max |H| = 1.0000 at offset 0, max sum|taps| = 1.25 (docs/prior-art.md §1.3). | falsifier: a time-varying (modulated) read in which the kernel's offset sweep pumps loop energy above unity despite static |H| ≤ 1 — the open item in prior-art §9. | supersedes: —

<a id="L0003"></a>
[L0003] Linear interpolation's per-repeat HF loss depends on the sample rate, so it breaks SR-independence in a feedback loop | candidate | added: 2026-10-19 | tags: sample-rate-independence, time-accuracy | lesson: At a half-sample offset linear interpolation is |cos(ω/2)|, re-applied on every pass of the loop. At 20 kHz that is −16.7 dB per repeat at 44.1 kHz, −11.7 dB at 48 kHz, −2.0 dB at 96 kHz and −0.47 dB at 192 kHz, so the same patch darkens at different rates on different hosts, and differently again depending on whether the delay lands on a whole sample. Measure per-repeat loss at offsets 0 and 0.5 across rates before choosing a kernel. | evidence: computed by the lead, 2026-10-19 (docs/prior-art.md §1.2); J. O. Smith, PASP "Delay-Line Interpolation" (amplitude error audible in feedback loops). | falsifier: Echo's lines run at a fixed internal rate, or the loop read is kept integer-aligned. | supersedes: —

<a id="L0004"></a>
[L0004] Bound the loop's filter path, not the feedback knob, when past-unity feedback is a feature | candidate | added: 2026-10-20 | tags: feedback-stability | lesson: A stability invariant phrased as "loop gain < 1 at every reachable setting" silently forbids the deliberate past-unity regime (feedback > 1 parked by a ceiling on the returning term). State it as peak |H_loop| ≤ 1 (filters, shelves, resonance, interpolation kernel never ADD gain), so loop gain ≤ g; then g > 1 is a declared regime the ceiling owns, with an infinite tail. Cross-check any loop-gain invariant against the ceiling's oracle row before ratifying it. | evidence: D-005 as proposed in Phase 0 said "< 1 for every reachable parameter combination" and was ratified by poll before the lead caught that it contradicted ADR-142 (intake/legacy/ADR-142-standard-delay.md:32) and D-000 Q4's "ceiling holds past unity"; amended by D-013. | falsifier: Echo's spec caps feedback below unity (then the two phrasings coincide). | supersedes: —

<a id="L0006"></a>
[L0006] The textbook DC blocker has gain 2/(1+R) > 1 at Nyquist — normalise it before it sits in a bounded loop | candidate | added: 2026-10-20 | tags: feedback-stability | lesson: H(z) = (1 − z⁻¹)/(1 − R z⁻¹) is a highpass whose magnitude rises monotonically to 2/(1+R) at Nyquist, slightly above 1 (1.0003 at 5 Hz, 48 kHz). In a feedback loop whose invariant is peak |H_loop| ≤ 1 it breaches the bound on its own. Multiply by (1+R)/2: the magnitude is then ≤ 1 everywhere and exactly 1 at Nyquist. Its impulse gain is then (1+R)/2, which is what a "generation ratio = feedback" test must expect. | evidence: spec/SPEC.md §5.1 and §5.3; tools/lab_smoke.mjs check 8 measures generation 2 = g(1+R)/2 = 0.499836 at 48 kHz, g = 0.5. | falsifier: the loop bound is relaxed to allow gain above 1 at frequencies no filter passes (then the textbook form is harmless). | supersedes: —

<a id="L0007"></a>
[L0007] A click probe commensurate with the test delays is blind to jumps — pick an incommensurate frequency | candidate | added: 2026-10-20 | tags: time-accuracy, modulation-repitch | lesson: A jump between two read points of a steady sine is invisible when the delay difference is a whole number of periods: 440 Hz × 0.25 s = 110 cycles, so a 0.5 → 0.25 s bare jump read M2 0.25 ("clean") on a 440 Hz probe and 36.6 on 431.7 Hz. Any click detector's probe must be incommensurate with every test time, and its must-fail plant must use the same probe and move as the real check. | evidence: lab measurements 2026-10-20 (Phase A trace); tools/lab_smoke.mjs check 12 and lab ECHO_PROBE_HZ. | falsifier: a detector that does not use a steady periodic probe (an analytic-residual metric such as D-009 M1). | supersedes: —

<a id="L0008"></a>
[L0008] A modulation ramp's length is a read speed — derive it from the depth, not from the gain-ramp default | candidate | added: 2026-10-20 | tags: modulation-repitch | lesson: Ramping a delay-modulation depth (or a stereo spread, or a shape blend) moves the read position by up to the depth over the ramp, so its speed change is depth/ramp: 20 ms of depth over B435's 20 ms gain ramp moves the head at 2× (a pitch blip; reverse-capable at larger depths). Size modulation ramps from the read-speed budget (250 ms keeps a 20 ms depth within 8 %), and glide the rate so a slope cap that depends on it moves smoothly. Likewise, D-009's M2 reads a speed kink (a step in the first difference) at a few, between smooth glides (≤ 1) and jumps (tens). | evidence: spec/SPEC.md §7 and §15; lab measurements (slew and log-one-pole laws M2 4.1, crit2 0.43). | falsifier: modulation applied as a crossfade between two fixed taps rather than a moving read. | supersedes: —

[L0009] Once a module can rest, a check fed silence can pass by measuring nothing — prove each run stayed awake | candidate | added: 2026-10-20 | tags: time-accuracy, modulation-repitch | lesson: Adding a rest state (a tail gate after which the module holds still and snaps parameters) silently turned impulse-driven checks vacuous: the module went to rest within a block, every later "move" snapped, and the read-speed check passed while exercising nothing — its plant (modulation slew limit removed) could not fire.
