- **Queue item:** T-046 (criteria in ROADMAP; criteria 4 - 6 and the scope added by D-050 A4, the human's five poll answers).
- **Why:** T-044's critic found a key move or a `time.mode` flip landing on a fast-moving tempo reading over the click bar at 192 kHz (D-050 A3); the human ruled it fixed before the listen (T-023).
- **Evidence consulted:** CLAUDE.md §Domain; ROADMAP T-044, T-046; D-049 A4 - A10, D-050 A3; SPEC §3.5, §3.8, §13; `engine.h` `stepHead`, `latch`, `startFade`, `outgoingSlew`; `core_tests.cpp` `stepClick`, `clickRun`, `worstAtRates`; the lab's `_headStep`; T-044's critic's probe source (its transcript), for the exact gesture.

- **The mechanism:** a key's glide follows `time.change` until the head comes within one sample of its moving target, then `time.tempoMode` owns it, and the head arrives still moving (speed up to 2.0). Crossfade started no fade under half a sample, so the head stopped where it stood, kept a stale speed, and stepped again when the next fade picked that speed up; follow put it on the grid by the cap's own step. A speed step reads in proportion to the rate.
- **What changed:**
  - `engine.h` `stepHead`: a moving head (`h.V != 0`) under crossfade / jump, or under follow past the cap, leaves through a fade; follow's in-cap branch also requires the law's speed inside the cap, both sides, and clears the speed once the law is over. At a fade's end, if no key moved the target during it (`Head::keyQueued`), `time.tempoMode` takes over (D-050 A4 ruling 4). A tempo tick under a key's move in flight no longer re-latches it (ruling 5).
  - `spec/SPEC.md` §3.5 (under half a sample starts no fade only for a head at rest; the comb cost of such a fade; the deferral on a moving tempo), §3.8 (the handover and its limit), the header.
